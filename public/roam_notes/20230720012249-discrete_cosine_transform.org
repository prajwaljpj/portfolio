#+title: discrete cosine transform

The Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) is a technique used to transform a signal or image from the spatial domain to the frequency domain. It is widely used in various applications, such as image and video compression, audio processing, and data compression.

The DCT is based on the idea that most real-world signals, including images and audio, have a significant amount of energy concentrated in low-frequency components, while high-frequency components carry less energy. By transforming the signal into the frequency domain, it becomes possible to represent the signal using fewer coefficients, thereby achieving compression.

The DCT works by decomposing the signal into a sum of cosine functions of different frequencies, each multiplied by a coefficient that represents the amplitude of that frequency component in the signal. The resulting coefficients, which represent the frequency spectrum of the signal, can be quantized and encoded to reduce the amount of data required for storage or transmission.

There are several types of DCT, with the most commonly used variant being the Discrete Cosine Transform Type-II (DCT-II). This variant is often referred to simply as DCT. It is a real-valued transform, meaning that both the input and output signals are real numbers. The DCT-II operates on a block of input data, usually a square block of pixels in the case of image compression.

The DCT is a reversible transformation, meaning that the original signal can be perfectly reconstructed from its DCT coefficients by applying the inverse DCT operation. This makes it suitable for lossless compression techniques. However, in lossy compression applications, such as JPEG image compression, quantization is typically applied to the DCT coefficients, resulting in a loss of some information.
