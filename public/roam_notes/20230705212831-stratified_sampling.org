#+title: stratified sampling

Stratified sampling is a sampling technique used in statistics to select a representative sample from a population with different subgroups or strata. In this method, the population is divided into distinct groups or strata based on certain characteristics that are of interest to the researcher. 

The goal of stratified sampling is to ensure that each stratum is represented in the sample in proportion to its size in the population. This is done by selecting a random sample from each stratum, where the size of the sample from each stratum is proportional to the size of the stratum in the population. 

By using stratified sampling, researchers can improve the accuracy and reliability of the results by ensuring that the sample is representative of the population as a whole. It helps to take into account the variability and diversity within the population and ensures that important subgroups are adequately represented in the sample. 

This sampling technique is commonly used when the population has distinct characteristics or when there is a desire to specifically examine a certain subgroup within the population. For example, if a researcher wants to study the income distribution among different age groups within a city, they can divide the population into age-based strata and select random samples from each stratum to get a representative sample.
