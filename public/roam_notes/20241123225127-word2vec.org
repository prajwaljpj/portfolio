#+title: Word2Vec
#+filetags: :paper:LLM:

The Word2Vec paper, titled "Distributed Representations of Words and Their Compositionality," was published by Tomas Mikolov et al. in 2013. It introduces a novel approach to generating word embeddings, which are dense vector representations of words designed to capture their meanings and relationships with other words. Here’s a detailed breakdown:

*** Key Concepts:

1. *Word Embeddings*:
   - The goal of Word2Vec is to create word embeddings that represent words in continuous vector space, allowing for semantic relationships to be captured mathematically.

2. *Skip-Gram and Continuous Bag of Words (CBOW)*:
   - *Skip-Gram*: This model predicts context words given a target word. It is particularly effective for capturing the meaning of words in different contexts.
   - *CBOW*: This model predicts a target word from surrounding context words. CBOW is faster but can be less accurate than Skip-Gram for infrequent words.

3. *Architecture*:
   - Both models use shallow neural networks, which consist of an input layer (representing the target or context words), a hidden layer (which learns the embeddings), and an output layer (representing predicted word probabilities).
   - The networks have two sets of weights: one for input to hidden layer and one for hidden to output layer.

4. *Loss Function*:
   - The models utilize [[id:0b98eebb-0a83-4547-8181-680c73847dc2][softmax]] or negative sampling to optimize the calculations of word probabilities efficiently, crucial due to the vast vocabulary size.

5. *Training Process*:
   - The training involves using a large corpus of text. Words in a context window around a target word are used to train the model, adjusting the weights using [[id:95fca8e7-de01-4056-8682-1ba683dc2a88][stochastic gradient descent]].

6. *Evaluation*:
   - The quality of the embeddings is often evaluated through word analogy tasks (e.g., "king - man + woman = queen") and word similarity tests.

*** Key Results:
- Word2Vec significantly reduces the dimensionality of word representations, typically to 100-300 dimensions while retaining useful semantic information.
- It captures various linguistic relationships, such as synonyms, analogies, and even some syntactic nuances.
