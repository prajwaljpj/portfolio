#+title: Regression

Regression is a statistical method used to model and analyze the relationships between variables. It aims to predict the value of a dependent variable based on one or more independent variables.

*** Types of Regression
1. *Linear Regression*: Models the relationship with a linear equation.
   - *Simple Linear Regression*:
     \[
     y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x + \epsilon
     \]
     Where:
     - \(y\) = dependent variable
     - \(x\) = independent variable
     - \(\beta_0\) = y-intercept
     - \(\beta_1\) = slope of the line
     - \(\epsilon\) = error term

   - *Multiple Linear Regression*:
     \[
     y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_1 + \beta_2 x_2 + \ldots + \beta_n x_n + \epsilon
     \]

*** Mathematical Foundations
- *Least Squares Method*: This technique minimizes the sum of the squares of the residuals (the differences between observed and predicted values).
   \[
   \text{Minimize } \sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2
   \]
   where \(\hat{y}_i\) is the predicted value from the regression model.

*** Estimation of Coefficients
- The coefficients \(\beta\) are usually estimated using:
   \[
   \hat{\beta} = (X^TX)^{-1}X^Ty
   \]
   where \(X\) is the matrix of independent variables (including a column of ones for the intercept), and \(y\) is the vector of observed values.

*** Assumptions of Linear Regression
1. Linearity
2. Independence
3. [[id:2e9dddd3-d359-44f8-8768-dc7abc285edc][Homoscedasticity]] (constant variance of errors)
4. Normality of errors

Regression analysis helps understand relationships in data and make predictions.
