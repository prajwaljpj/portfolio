#+title: Hidden Markov Models (HMM)

* Intro
A Hidden Markov Model (HMM) is a statistical model that represents systems which are assumed to be a Markov process with hidden states. HMMs are widely used in time series analysis and are applicable in various fields, such as speech recognition, bioinformatics, and more.

1. **States**:
   - Let \( S = \{S_1, S_2, \ldots, S_N\} \) be a set of hidden states.

2. **Observations**:
   - Let \( O = \{O_1, O_2, \ldots, O_T\} \) be a sequence of observations from the observable space.

3. **Transition Probabilities**:
   - Define the state transition probability matrix \( A \) where
     \( A_{ij} = P(S_t = S_j | S_{t-1} = S_i) \).
     This matrix describes the probability of transitioning from state \( S_i \) to state \( S_j \).

4. **Emission Probabilities**:
   - Define the emission probability matrix \( B \) where
     \( B_{ij} = P(O_t = O_j | S_t = S_i) \).
     This matrix provides the probabilities of generating observation \( O_j \) from state \( S_i \).

5. **Initial State Distribution**:
   - Let \( \pi \) be the initial state distribution where \( \pi_i = P(S_1 = S_i) \).

** Mathematical Formalism**

An HMM is defined by the triplet \( \lambda = (A, B, \pi) \).

1. **Forward Algorithm**:
   To compute the probability of an observed sequence \( O \):

   \[
   \alpha_t(i) = \sum_{j=1}^{N} \alpha_{t-1}(j) A_{ji} B_{iO_t}
   \]
   Where \( \alpha_t(i) \) is the probability of observing the first \( t \) observations and being in state \( S_i \) at time \( t \).

   The probability of the observation sequence \( O \) is given by:

   \[
   P(O | \lambda) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_T(i)
   \]

2. **Viterbi Algorithm**:
   To find the most likely sequence of hidden states \( S \):

   \[
   \delta_t(i) = \max_{j} (\delta_{t-1}(j) A_{ji}) \times B_{iO_t}
   \]
   Where \( \delta_t(i) \) is the maximum probability of the state sequence leading to state \( S_i \).

3. **Baum-Welch Algorithm**:
   To estimate the parameters \( A \), \( B \), and \( \pi \) given a set of observed sequences.

   - This is an Expectation-Maximization method that iteratively optimizes the parameters to maximize the likelihood of the observed sequences.

** Applications of HMM**

- **Speech Recognition**: Modeling phonemes.
- **Bioinformatics**: Gene prediction.
- **Finance**: Modeling hidden market states.

** Conclusion**

HMMs provide a powerful framework for modeling temporal sequences where the underlying processes are hidden. Understanding how to implement and apply these models can yield insights across various disciplines.
